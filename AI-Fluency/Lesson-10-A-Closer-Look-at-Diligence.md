### 📌 What Is This About?

This lesson examines the **Diligence** competency — the ethical and safety dimension of AI Fluency. While the other three Ds (Delegation, Description, Discernment) primarily address effectiveness and efficiency, Diligence focuses on ensuring that your AI use is rigorous, transparent, and accountable. It introduces three types: **Creation Diligence, Transparency Diligence, and Deployment Diligence**.

---

### 💡 Why Does This Matter?

In IT, governance frameworks like ITIL, ISO 27001, and GDPR exist not because most IT professionals intend to cause harm, but because well-intentioned actions in complex systems can have unintended consequences — and accountability structures prevent and address those consequences.

Diligence is the AI governance layer. It asks you to think beyond "does this work?" to "is this responsible?" — and to own the answer.

---

### 📖 The Story

Diligence recognises a fundamental truth: **AI systems and our interactions with them do not exist in a vacuum.** There are always broader contexts, other people affected, and responsibilities attached to the work you create with AI.

Think of it this way — when driving, you do not just focus on getting from A to B efficiently. You follow traffic rules, maintain awareness of other road users, and take responsibility for your driving decisions. Diligence brings that same responsible awareness to AI collaboration.

#### The Central Question of Diligence

Before, during, and after any AI interaction, Diligence asks:
- *What are the broader implications of working with this AI?*
- *Who might be affected by what is created, or by the collaboration itself?*
- *Who has access to the data I am inputting?*
- *How do I ensure my interaction and its outcome align with ethical standards and my organisation's values?*

#### Sub-Concept 1: Creation Diligence

**Creation Diligence** is being critical and intentional about *which AI systems you choose to work with* and *how you work with them*.

Before engaging with any AI tool professionally, ask:

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| How is this system trained and what data was used? | Training data biases can appear in outputs |
| Who owns the data I input right now? | Determines IP rights and data control |
| Who may have access to it once I share it? | Privacy and confidentiality implications |
| Does this platform have appropriate data protection policies? | Compliance with regulations and company policy |
| Does this interaction align with my organisation's AI usage policy? | Professional responsibility |
| What are the environmental and societal impacts of using this system? | Broader ethical awareness |

**Practical example:** Before sharing a client's financial data with an AI tool to help prepare an analysis, you must verify the platform's data retention policy, whether your company has an enterprise agreement that prevents data training on your inputs, and whether sharing this data with an external AI service complies with your NDA with the client.

**This is not optional for professionals.** The legal and regulatory environment around AI is still evolving, but your professional responsibilities to clients and your organisation apply regardless.

#### Sub-Concept 2: Transparency Diligence

**Transparency Diligence** is being open and accurate about AI's role in your work with all stakeholders who need to know.

Different professional contexts have different expectations about AI disclosure:
- **Academic settings:** Strong expectations of original work; AI assistance must typically be declared
- **Creative professional settings:** Growing norms around disclosing AI involvement in client deliverables
- **Corporate/professional settings:** Many organisations have policies requiring disclosure when AI was used in client-facing or regulatory submissions
- **Personal use:** Fewer formal obligations, but ethical considerations still apply

The key questions for Transparency Diligence:
- *Who needs to know about AI's role in this work?*
- *How and when should I communicate this?*
- *What level of detail is appropriate to disclose?*

**Meeting transparency expectations is not just about compliance — it is about maintaining trust and respect in professional relationships.** People have the right to know when AI has played a significant role in content creation or in decisions that affect them.

**Practical example:** If you use Claude to help draft a team proposal, letting your colleagues know which sections were AI-assisted allows for more honest collaboration. If you submit an AI-assisted report to a client without disclosure, and that is later discovered, the damage to the professional relationship can be significant.

#### Sub-Concept 3: Deployment Diligence

**Deployment Diligence** is taking informed responsibility for the outputs you use or share after they have been created with AI assistance.

When you share AI-generated content with others — colleagues, clients, the public — **you, not the AI, are responsible for its accuracy and appropriateness**.

This means:
- **Verifying facts** — especially specific numbers, dates, citations, and technical claims
- **Checking for bias** — particularly in anything that affects people (HR decisions, performance evaluations, customer communications)
- **Ensuring compliance** — with legal, regulatory, and organisational standards that would apply if you had written it yourself
- **Confirming usage rights** — for AI-generated creative content that may have IP implications

> **The standard is clear: the quality bar for AI-assisted work must be the same as the quality bar for human-created work.** The fact that AI helped you does not lower the threshold.

**Practical example:** If a journalist uses AI to help draft an article, they must verify every fact and source before publishing — exactly as they would if they had written it entirely themselves. The AI's involvement does not transfer any responsibility. The journalist's name is on the byline; the journalist owns the accountability.

#### Developing Your Personal AI Ethics Guidelines

Because the legal and regulatory framework around AI is still evolving and varies by jurisdiction, the course recommends developing:
1. **Personal guidelines** for working with AI that align with your own professional ethics
2. **Organisational awareness** — knowing your company's AI usage policies and which AI tools are approved
3. **Industry awareness** — understanding any sector-specific regulations or standards that apply (healthcare, finance, legal, etc.)
4. **Ongoing learning** — staying current as the regulatory environment evolves

### 📊 Key Concepts Snapshot

| Type of Diligence | Core Focus | Key Questions |
|------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Creation Diligence** | Which AI systems to use and how to work with them | Data ownership? Platform policies? Organisational approval? |
| **Transparency Diligence** | Being open about AI's role in your work | Who needs to know? When and how to disclose? |
| **Deployment Diligence** | Taking responsibility for AI-assisted outputs you share | Is this accurate? Compliant? Bias-checked? Can I stand behind it? |

**Core principle:** The Diligence mindset = *"I am accountable for everything I create with AI, to the same professional standard as work I created entirely myself."*

### 🏢 Real-World Scenario

**Situation:** Your IT company is preparing a Managed Security Services proposal for a potential enterprise client. A senior pre-sales consultant uses Claude to draft the technical section.

**Creation Diligence:**
- The consultant checks whether Claude.ai (or whichever tool is used) is on the company's approved AI tools list ✓
- The company has an enterprise agreement with Anthropic that prevents training on business inputs ✓
- The consultant does NOT paste client-specific data (the prospect's current security posture or known vulnerabilities) into the prompt — this could be confidential information shared under NDA ✓
- The consultant keeps all prompt inputs at the generic capability-description level, not client-specific ✓

**Transparency Diligence:**
- The company's proposal process requires a note in the internal document log that AI tools were used in drafting ✓
- The final proposal does not require external disclosure of AI use under current client requirements, but the consultant makes a note in the CRM for future reference ✓
- The senior manager reviewing the proposal is informed it was AI-drafted for additional scrutiny ✓

**Deployment Diligence:**
- The consultant carefully reviews all technical specifications Claude generated — a few had outdated certifications that needed updating ✓
- Pricing and SLA figures are verified against the company's current rate card (not Claude's suggestion) ✓
- Competitive comparisons Claude included are fact-checked against current market data ✓
- The consultant personally signs off on the technical section — they own it professionally ✓

**Result:** A high-quality proposal delivered faster, with full professional accountability, appropriate governance, and no compliance exposure.

### ⚠️ Watch Out For

**Trap 1: Treating data security as someone else's problem**
When you paste data into an AI tool, you are making a data governance decision — whether you realise it or not. "I did not know the platform stored my inputs" is not a defence when sensitive data is exposed. Creation Diligence requires proactive verification.

**Trap 2: Confusing "adding a disclosure note" with Transparency Diligence**
Transparency Diligence is about genuinely understanding who needs to know about AI involvement and ensuring they do — not just adding a boilerplate AI disclaimer. Who are the stakeholders? How does AI's involvement affect their trust in the work?

**Trap 3: Assuming AI-generated content meets a lower bar**
AI-generated content is not a draft that "just needs a quick review." It requires the same thorough review you would apply to any professional deliverable — including fact-checking, bias review, compliance verification, and personal sign-off.

**Trap 4: Waiting for regulations to tell you what to do**
The regulatory framework around AI is evolving, but your professional ethical obligations exist now. Do not wait for a specific AI regulation to apply your existing professional standards of honesty, accuracy, and accountability to your AI-assisted work.

### ✅ Quick Check

**Q1.** An HR manager uses Claude to help draft performance review templates. She pastes anonymised employee performance data (names removed but roles, teams, and specific performance details included) into Claude to help it generate more context-specific templates. What Diligence concern does this raise?

- A) No concern — the data was anonymised, so there is no privacy risk
- B) Transparency Diligence — she should tell employees their data was used
- C) Creation Diligence — even anonymised employee performance data may be identifiable and sensitive; she should verify the platform's data policies and her company's AI usage policy before inputting this data
- D) Deployment Diligence — she needs to review the templates carefully before using them

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal answer</summary>

**Correct Answer: C**

This is a **Creation Diligence** issue. Anonymisation does not eliminate all privacy risk — in small teams, role + team + performance details can be re-identifiable. More importantly, employee performance data is sensitive HR data that most organisations have specific policies about how to handle. Before inputting this data into an external AI platform, she must verify (a) the platform's data retention and training policies, (b) her company's AI usage policy for HR data, and (c) whether this complies with applicable employment data protection regulations. Option A is incorrect — anonymisation is not a blanket data protection measure.

**Q2.** A software engineering team lead uses Claude to help write a critical security architecture document and does not disclose this to the security review committee that will evaluate it. The committee approves the document, partially based on its apparent thoroughness. What Diligence principle was violated?

- A) Creation Diligence — the wrong AI tool was used for a security document
- B) Transparency Diligence — stakeholders (the review committee) who needed to know about AI's role in producing the document were not informed
- C) Deployment Diligence — the document was not thoroughly reviewed before submission
- D) No violation — AI assistance in document drafting does not need to be disclosed

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal answer</summary>

**Correct Answer: B**

This is a **Transparency Diligence** violation. The security review committee is a key stakeholder who needed to know that the document was AI-assisted — this information could affect how thoroughly they scrutinise it and whether they apply additional verification steps. Transparency Diligence recognises that people have the right to know when AI has played a significant role in work that affects decisions they make. The fact that the document may be technically accurate does not eliminate the transparency obligation.

</details>

**Q3.** A legal team uses Claude to draft a summary of regulatory compliance requirements for a client briefing. The summary is reviewed once for obvious errors and then sent to the client. Two weeks later, the client discovers that one of the regulatory requirements cited was based on outdated legislation. What Diligence principle was most directly violated?

- A) Creation Diligence — the wrong AI tool was used for legal work
- B) Transparency Diligence — the client was not told AI was used
- C) Deployment Diligence — the team failed to verify the accuracy of the regulatory information before sharing it, applying a lower quality bar than would be expected for professional legal work
- D) No Diligence violation — AI-generated content being slightly outdated is acceptable

<details>
<summary>👉 Click to reveal answer</summary>

**Correct Answer: C**

This is a **Deployment Diligence** failure. The team applied a lower quality bar to AI-generated content than they would apply to professionally researched legal work. Regulatory information is exactly the kind of factual, specific, high-stakes content that requires independent verification — especially given LLMs' knowledge cutoff limitations and hallucination tendencies. Deployment Diligence requires taking full professional responsibility for AI-assisted output, including the same rigorous fact-checking you would apply to content you wrote entirely yourself.

</details>
