# Lesson 2 — Your First Conversation with Claude

> **Estimated time:** 20 minutes

---

### 📌 What Is This About?

This lesson is hands-on. You learn how to actually start talking to Claude — how to navigate the interface, how to write a good prompt, how to upload files for context, and how to go back-and-forth with Claude to get a better result. By the end, you should be comfortable opening Claude.ai and having a real, useful conversation.

---

### 💡 Why Does This Matter?

Most people's first conversation with an AI tool is disappointing simply because they don't know how to "talk" to it properly. A vague question gets a vague answer. This lesson gives you a simple, repeatable framework so that from day one, your prompts are clear and your results are useful — instead of wasting time on trial and error.

---

### 📖 The Explanation

#### Key takeaways from the "Getting Started" video

- Claude is a powerful, intelligent collaborator that amplifies your capability across all your work. **Claude brings the AI intelligence — you bring the context and expertise** that makes the work meaningful.
- Speak to Claude like you would speak to a coworker — naturally, concisely, and conversationally. No need for special "magic words."
- Before starting a conversation, think of three things: **setting the stage** (your role, objective, context), **defining the task** (the action you want), and **specifying rules** (style, tone, examples).
- When you upload documents or background info into a chat, Claude considers that content while responding — think of it as a shortcut so Claude understands your needs faster.
- The real power of Claude shows up with **continued, frequent** communication — not one-off prompts.

#### Starting your first conversation

When you open Claude.ai, you see a clean interface with a text box at the bottom. Your prompts can range from something as simple as brainstorming a code name for a new feature, to something complex like asking Claude to co-create an entire file with you.

The sidebar has a few useful icons:
- **New chat / chat history** — start fresh or jump back into old conversations.
- **Projects** — organise conversations with persistent context and custom instructions (covered in a later lesson).
- **Artifacts** — turn ideas into shareable apps, tools, or content (also covered later).

#### Writing effective prompts — the 3-part framework

All interactions with Claude begin with a prompt, and this prompt (combined with any other context) shapes Claude's response. Before your next conversation, think about:

1. **Setting the stage** — What is your role? What are your objectives? Is there context about your work Claude should know?
2. **Defining the task** — What action do you want Claude to take? Write, analyse, build, something else?
3. **Specifying rules** — What style or tone do you want? Do you have examples you can share to show Claude what "good" looks like?

**Example prompt using all three parts:**

> "I'm the marketing lead at an indie streaming startup, and we're preparing an investor pitch deck for Series A investors. Can you research the current state of the independent film streaming market and identify key trends, competitor positioning, and growth opportunities? Use current web research with citations and structure it as a professional report of up to 5 pages, with an executive summary, market analysis, competitive landscape, and growth opportunities."

Breaking it down:
- **Setting the stage** — marketing lead, investor pitch deck context.
- **Defining the task** — research the market: trends, competitors, opportunities.
- **Specifying rules** — current web research with citations, structured as a professional report.

> **Good to know:** This 3-part prompt framework is adapted from the **4D Framework for AI Fluency** — developed by Prof. Rick Dakan (Ringling College of Art and Design) and Prof. Joseph Feller (University College Cork). The 4 Ds are **Delegation, Description, Discernment, and Diligence**. Anthropic has a free AI Fluency course covering all four in depth — worth exploring if you enjoy this deeper thinking style.

#### Adding context — uploads, connectors, and preferences

Uploads, connectors, and custom preferences give Claude even more context about your work.

- Claude can read both **text and visual elements** (images, charts, graphics) inside PDFs and other documents. Supported file types include PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, and common image formats like PNG and JPEG.
- Practical uses: upload a document and ask for a summary; share an image and ask Claude to describe/analyse it; attach a spreadsheet and ask for trends; upload code and ask Claude to explain it or find bugs.
- Once uploaded, Claude automatically parses the file; it shows up as an attachment in chat, and you can then ask questions about it.
- **Pro-tip:** Go to Settings > General > "What personal preferences should Claude consider?" to set preferences that apply to *every* conversation — no need to repeat yourself each time.

#### Iterating on Claude's responses

Conversations are meant to be **iterative**. If Claude's first response is not quite right, you have options:

- **Ask follow-up questions** — "Can you expand on the second point?" or "That's helpful, but can you make it more concise?"
- **Give feedback** — "This is good, but the tone is too formal. Can you make it more conversational?"
- **Redirect or restart** — "Actually, I was asking about X, not Y. Let me clarify..." If a conversation has really gone sideways, it may be faster to start a fresh chat.
- **Pro-tip:** Click the pencil icon on any of your own messages to edit and resubmit it — this is handy when you want to refine a request rather than pile on a new message.

#### Personalising Claude

There are two features that make Claude work better for you over time:

- **Memory** — automatically saves key context from your conversations (your role, preferences, past decisions, working style), so you don't repeat yourself every time you start a new chat. You can review, edit, or delete anything Claude remembers in Settings, and memory syncs across all your devices.
- **Styles** — let you customise how Claude communicates. Choose a preset (concise, formal, explanatory) or create a custom style describing exactly how you want Claude to write. Once set, it applies across all your conversations automatically.

#### From the video — model selector, extended thinking, and Research (bonus detail)

- The **search and tools menu** gives Claude access to more tools to pull in context as it needs — for example, toggling on **Web Search** for current market data, or connecting a data source like Google Drive for real-time information. Claude picks the right approach automatically.
- The **model selector** appears under the text box. Click on the model name to change it (note: changing the model starts a new chat). Options include:
  - **Claude Opus** — the largest-scale model with hybrid reasoning; best for your most complex tasks (e.g. multi-step financial analysis).
  - **Claude Sonnet** — the recommended default for everyday use; balances sophisticated capability with cost-effectiveness ("the right tool for the job"). You can switch models anytime.
  - **Extended thinking mode** — valuable for complex thinking/analysis, but adds latency; not needed for simple, everyday questions. Tip: start with Sonnet and thinking off for the fastest results on simple tasks, and turn on thinking (or switch to Opus) when you need more support.
  - **Research** — lets Claude conduct a systematic, multi-angle investigation, automatically breaking down complex questions and exploring many sources to deliver a comprehensive, cited report in 5 to 45 minutes.
- Closing message from the video: the real power of Claude comes from **continued and frequent communication** — the more you interact with it, the better it gets at understanding your style and preferences.

### 📊 Key Concepts Snapshot

| Term | Meaning | Why It Matters |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **3-part prompt framework** | Setting the stage + Defining the task + Specifying rules | Turns a vague ask into a clear, well-shaped request |
| **Uploads / context** | Documents, images, spreadsheets you share with Claude | Gives Claude the background it needs, without you re-typing everything |
| **Memory** | Claude's ability to remember your context across chats | Saves time — you don't repeat your role/preferences every time |
| **Styles** | Presets or custom descriptions of how Claude should write | Keeps tone/voice consistent across all conversations |
| **Model selector** | Choice between Opus, Sonnet, extended thinking, Research | Lets you match the "engine" to how hard the task really is |
| **Iteration** | Following up, giving feedback, or redirecting Claude | The first response is a draft, not the final answer |

### 🏢 Real-World Scenario

**Situation:** You need Claude to help you draft a client email about a delayed project.

**Weak approach:** "Write an email about the delay." — You get a generic, forgettable email.

**Strong approach (using the 3-part framework):** "I'm the account manager for an enterprise client (setting the stage). Write an email explaining that our software integration will be delayed by two weeks — this is the second delay and the client has been patient so far (defining the task). Keep it professional but apologetic, and end with a clear next step (specifying rules)." You then read the draft, ask Claude to "make paragraph two shorter" (iteration), and you have a polished email in two turns instead of twenty minutes of typing.
